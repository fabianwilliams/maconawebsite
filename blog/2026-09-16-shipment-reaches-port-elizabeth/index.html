The Summer 2026 Shipment Reaches the Port, Bound for the Atlantic

On September 16, 2026, all three GPS trackers in our Summer 2026 shipment pinged together at the Port of New York and New Jersey in Elizabeth. This is the last checkpoint on US soil before the container begins its ocean crossing to Mali.

Live tracker available: See where this shipment is right now →

What happened today

Two weeks ago, three bags of donations left a Philadelphia warehouse for loading. Today the trackers told us where they went next. All three pinged together at the Port of New York and New Jersey, the busiest container port on the East Coast, in and around Elizabeth, New Jersey. MACONA-TKR-2 checked in along the New Jersey Turnpike in Linden at 8:13 this morning, and MACONA-TKR-3 surfaced a few hours later right at the Elizabeth marine terminal on Newark Bay, container yards on every side.

This is the last checkpoint on US soil. From this terminal the container is loaded onto an ocean vessel and begins the crossing to West Africa, then travels overland toward Mali for distribution. Seeing all three trackers arrive at the port together is exactly the confirmation we want before the container goes to sea.

MACONA-TKR-3 pinging from the container yards at the Elizabeth marine terminal, right on Newark Bay. This is where the container meets the ship.

MACONA-TKR-2 along the New Jersey Turnpike in Linden, with all three trackers clustered around the port. You do not have to take our word for it, you can watch the same map we do.

Why the port matters, and why dollars keep it moving

Reaching the port is the moment the invisible costs begin. Ocean freight to landlocked Mali is slow, roughly three months door to door, and getting a container onto a ship and off again is where donated dollars do the quiet work: port fees, customs clearance, and safe last-mile transport once the container lands in West Africa. In-kind donations fill the bags. Dollar donations move them across the ocean.

That is why overlapping shipments matter. Keeping a container leaving on a steady cadence, rather than one big push a year, is what turns your gift into consistent relief on the ground.
