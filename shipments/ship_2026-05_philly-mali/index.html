Journey Log

What has happened so far

Every event is timestamped and photographed. As trackers ping new locations, new events will appear here. Tip: click any photo to view it full size.

May 4, 2026 Maryland, USA Stage 1 · Collecting

5 bags packed in Maryland

Clothing, hygiene products, and over-the-counter medicine sorted across 5 shipping bags. 4 of the 5 bags carry Apple AirTags so we can see where they are at every land stop. The fifth bag is co-located and bundled with the others.

May 5, 2026 Philadelphia, PA Stage 2 · US port

Handoff at the Philadelphia container yard

Two hours each way from Maryland to Philadelphia to deliver the bags into the container destined for Mali. Trackers pinging strong inside the warehouse before the container is sealed. Read the original handoff post →

May 5, 2026 Philadelphia, PA warehouse Stage 2 · US port

Trackers verified active inside the container

Last confirmed pings before the container is sealed for ocean transit. 4 trackers all reading; bag inventory checked against the manifest.

June 3, 2026 Port Newark-Elizabeth, NJ Stage 3 · At sea (staging)

Trackers light up in New Jersey, staging at the port

After weeks quiet, the AirTags pinged again this morning as the container moved north through New Jersey: TT3 on the NJ Turnpike at Linden at 10:20, then TT4 in Elizabeth and TT-1 in Newark, all inside the Port Newark-Elizabeth complex. The container is staging at one of the busiest export ports on the East Coast for its ocean crossing to Mali.

June 5, 2026 Brooklyn, NY Stage 3 · US port staging

Trackers ping from Brooklyn's Red Hook Container Terminal

Two trackers surfaced again at 09:57 in Brooklyn, New York 11231, clustered around Red Hook Container Terminal and New York Harbor. The June 6 screenshots show MACONA-TT-1 and MACONA-TT2 still active, confirming the bags stayed trackable after the move from New Jersey into the Brooklyn port area.

July 17, 2026 Port of Dakar, Senegal Stage 4 · Africa port

It crossed the Atlantic — trackers surface in the Port of Dakar

After six quiet weeks at sea, the container is alive on the other side of the ocean. Two of the four trackers pinged in Dakar, Senegal on the afternoon of July 17: MACONA-TT-1 at 13:34 along Avenue Félix Eboué inside the port district, and MACONA-TT4 at 13:49 near the container terminal by Rue HB-17. This is the West Africa landfall we have been waiting for. MACONA-TT2 and TT3 are still quiet — normal for AirTags with no nearby iPhones — and we expect them to resurface as the bags move. Next stage: overland from Dakar toward Bamako, Mali for distribution.

July 23, 2026 N1 at M'Bour, Senegal Stage 4 · In Senegal

Out of the port and on the road: three of four trackers now reporting

The shipment is moving. On July 23, MACONA-TT-1 pinged at 17:10 and MACONA-TT2 at 18:02, both on the N1 national highway at M'Bour, a coastal town about 80 kilometers south of Dakar. A third tracker surfaced a short distance away, which brings us to three of the four now reporting, up from two at the Port of Dakar on July 17. Two of the trackers are traveling together and the third is close by. The bags are roughly 3,994 miles from where they were packed in Maryland. The quiet fourth tracker should resurface as the bags move through populated areas. The next stage is the overland leg to Bamako, Mali, where MACONA distributes the clothing, hygiene products, and over-the-counter medicine directly to families.

August 1, 2026 Kenieba, Mali Stage 5 · In country

It reached Mali, and the last silent tracker woke up

The shipment has crossed into its destination country. This morning at 04:39, MACONA-TT3 reported for the first time on the entire journey, and it did so in Kenieba, a town in western Mali near the Senegal border. That means all four trackers have now checked in at least once. The night before, at 20:00, MACONA-TT-1 traced the road ahead through Kenieba, Tambaga, Kita, and Kati, the overland route that leads to Bamako. The bags are not at the final destination yet. They are in Mali and on the road, with the last stretch running toward the capital, where MACONA distributes the clothing, hygiene products, and over-the-counter medicine directly to families.
